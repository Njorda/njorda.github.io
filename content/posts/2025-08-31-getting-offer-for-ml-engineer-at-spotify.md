# How I Prepared for My Spotify ML Engineer Interview and Got the Offer

This blog post goes over the process of how I prepared for my interviews at Spotify and ultimately got an offer for an ML engineer role. For some people, the interview process at FAANG-style companies is as easy as a walk in the park, but for me it required a lot of preparation. How much time you need is probably highly individual, but I really, really wanted the job so I went all in!

The interview process for me in short was:
1. Call with HR
2. A 1-hour "Machine Learning Tech Screen"
3. Assessment day with five interviews:
   - ML Data Interview 
   - Values
   - ML System Design 
   - ML Depth Interview

In the end I did an extra ML depth interview, but the normal process is probably the ones above.

For me, I broke the preparation down into two parts: the tech screen and assessment day, and did different preparations for each of them. For both of them I tried to Google and YouTube as much as possible to get inspiration for how the interview could look like. This was not to find the exact questions or a script of what to say, but rather more to get a feel for the interview and start thinking about how I would handle the questions. There are A LOT of videos to find out there; however, in hindsight, none of them really matched what I experienced at Spotify. In this post I will share how I prepared and my experience. I will not share any of the interview questions.

## The Tech Screen Preparations

For the tech screen part, I felt that my biggest gap was the coding problem I knew would be in the interview. I got information that the interview would be three parts: a short intro, technical ML questions, and finally a coding part. I felt confident in the first two parts so focused all my energy on the coding part.

There are a ton of different pages to use to train. I used two different ones - feel free to pick whatever you want, I believe there are a ton of good ones out there:
1. [Leetcode.com](https://leetcode.com/)
2. [AlgoExpert](https://www.algoexpert.io/product)

What was important for me was to be able to train on the same type of problems/algorithms in different ways. This was to learn the pattern rather than learn the exact questions. I also decided to focus on easy to medium ones; I never did any hard ones, to be honest.

For LeetCode I used the free path, but for AlgoExpert I paid, which fit me really well. I also tried to combine deep understanding with doing bulk problems - doing 1-3 a day for medium ones.

However, the most important part for me was asking friends for help. Solving the problem is one part of the problem, but doing it in front of another person and explaining at the same time is something totally different. I can highly recommend this - also ask for feedback from your friends. If you don't have any programming friends, that's fine. When you solve the problem, you should be able to explain it in a way so anyone can understand. For me, there was a really steep learning curve here and I felt a massive improvement after doing 5-ish.

If you're not comfortable, you need to read up on time and memory complexity. I know it sucks and is really boring, but they will ask and you will have to explain (at least that was my experience).

## The Assessment Day Preparations!

For this part of the process, I focused on three things: reading up on recommendation systems, ML system design, and reading articles (mainly from Spotify's blog).

I felt a need to freshen up on recommendation systems and spent a bit of time reading up on it. I did a mix of blogs, YouTube, and books. Some links are below:
- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vBRjGgdyTY
- https://www.hopsworks.ai/dictionary/two-tower-embedding-model
- https://www.uber.com/en-TW/blog/innovative-recommendation-applications-using-two-tower-embeddings/
- https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/scaling-deep-retrieval-tensorflow-two-towers-architecture
- https://medium.com/data-science/recommender-systems-a-complete-guide-to-machine-learning-models-96d3f94ea748

I focused on Content-Based, Collaborative Filtering Approaches, and the two-tower networks. In these sub-fields, I also read up on how real-time inputs would change the system. This part for me was self-study.

For reading articles, I did three things. I read articles from Spotify's blog that I felt were relevant. I also checked up on the interviewers I would meet at my assessment day and saw what they had published and read those articles/papers. Finally, I read the papers I found were the most defining in the role that I applied for (my ML role was in a specific area - most are - for example like gen-AI, recommendations, etc.). Some recommendations are:

- https://huggingface.co/blog
- https://blog.doordash.com/en-us
- https://engineering.atspotify.com/
- https://netflixtechblog.com/

The part of preparing I spent the most time on was ML system design. I started by reading [Machine Learning System Design Interview](https://www.amazon.com/Machine-Learning-System-Design-Interview/dp/1736049127). The reason I started with that was to set a recipe for how to break down an ML system design interview. There are numerous good ways to do it, and in the end I did a mix of the book and this YouTube channel [MLEpath](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XN2ymraj27g). I then had that in my back of my head during all the prep and future studies.

To get some hands-on experience, I did some fake interviews with friends (don't forget to time it). To get a fair experience, you need people that work in tech and the area you're applying for - doing it with a random friend working in accounting will probably not give you that much. When I felt that I was at a decent level, I used [Prepfully](https://prepfully.com/). It felt that doing the interview with a random person from a top company would give me a ton of experience and put me in the same feeling as doing it for real. In the end, I actually did two mock interviews from there. It wasn't cheap, but for me it was worth it. The feedback I got changed how I did my interviews and really helped me.

## So How Did the Interviews Feel?

The tech screening felt great for me. The coding challenge was a medium problem and I felt really comfortable when solving it.

The assessment day for me was another story - it was actually quite challenging. In hindsight, I should probably have prepared more for the data part, just like 5 hours or something. However, the systems design felt great - I felt I had done everything right and could relax and just go for it. The part that I really struggled with was the ML depth interview. I applied for a gen-AI role and some questions were really hard and I simply could not answer them. I actually failed the interview a lot because of the lacking knowledge in this specific part. However, after discussing with the HR person, I was invited to meet another team at a lower level (missed out on being senior). I did one more technical interview in an area that I was way more comfortable with and got an offer.
